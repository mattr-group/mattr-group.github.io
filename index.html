Getting more out of every readout.

MATTR is a magnetic resonance physics group in the Department of Bioengineering at Imperial College London. We design acquisition and encoding strategies that make MRI faster and sharper, and turn them into measurements that hold up outside our own scanner room.

Led by Dr Pete Lally · Sir Michael Uren Hub, White City Campus

About

What we work on and where we sit.

Most of MRI's remaining headroom is in how the signal is encoded, not in how it is cleaned up afterwards. We build pulse sequences and spatial encoding schemes that collect more information per unit time, and we validate them carefully enough that the resulting numbers can be used as biomarkers in a clinical study rather than only in a methods paper.

The group spans sequence design, ultra-high field imaging, diffusion microstructure, body MRI, and learning-based reconstruction. We work across Imperial's clinical MRI facilities and with collaborators in the NHS trusts, and we prefer projects where a physics idea has a named clinical question waiting for it.

How the week runs.

Conferences
ISMRM and the British Chapter meeting are the fixtures, alongside ISBI and GERF.

New members give an introduction at their first or second meeting: where you are from, how you got here, and what you are going to work on. It is the fastest way to find out who else in the room has already hit the problem you are about to hit.

Join us

We take PhD students through the Department of Bioengineering, and are happy to host MSc projects and visiting researchers. A background in physics, engineering, or a quantitative clinical science is the usual route in. Comfort with signal processing and a programming language matters more than previous MRI experience, and several people here arrived from clinical scientist training rather than from a physics PhD track.

If you are applying for your own fellowship or scholarship, get in touch early. Proposals are much stronger when the project is shaped together rather than fitted to a deadline.
